Sea service report (PDF)

Check with AMSA: for near coastal certificates AMSA accepts a Record of sea service (form 771), its sea service letter template signed by a supervisor/operator/owner/master/chief engineer, or an AMSA-approved task book. A logbook PDF is not on that list by itself. Form 771 sheets gives one page per vessel / duty / operation type with the details to transfer onto (or attach to) AMSA\u2019s official form 771 for your supervisor to sign. The Logbook PDF is the full record as supporting evidence.

Opens the print dialog. Choose Save as PDF (on iPhone/iPad: share icon → Save to Files; on Android: Save as PDF). The report includes blank lines for the Master / owner to sign and date.

How sea days are counted

Per AMSA near coastal rules: one day of sea service = 8 hours of work relevant to the certificate. Shifts shorter than 8 hours are added together until they reach 8 hours; more than 8 hours in a 24-hour period still counts as one day only. Time when the vessel is not underway (and not at anchor with someone on watch) is counted separately because AMSA caps it at 15% of the service required for a deck certificate (33% for engineering).

Import from spreadsheet

Bring in past records from Excel (.xlsx, .xls) or CSV. You choose which of your columns match which logbook fields, then check a preview before anything is added.

Excel files need an internet connection the first time to load the reader. CSV works offline.

Backup

Data is stored only on this device. If you clear browser data or lose the device it is gone, so export a backup regularly.
